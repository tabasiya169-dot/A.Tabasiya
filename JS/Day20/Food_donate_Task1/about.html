Mission & Technology

Bridging the Divide Between Surplus Food & Hunger

We believe that no safe, freshly prepared cooked meal should ever end up in a landfill when nearby neighbors, shelters, and care homes could thrive from it.

Our Core Purpose

A Hyperlocal Solution to a Global Challenge

Every celebration, restaurant service, university canteen, and household kitchen occasionally prepares more food than can be immediately consumed. Traditionally, logistical friction and lack of real-time communication resulted in edible food being discarded.

FoodBridge transforms this challenge into an opportunity. By pairing seamless front-end donor interactions with live open APIs and local coordination, we ensure that cooked meals, fresh bread, and fruits reach those who need them within safe consumption windows.

The 4-Step Lifeline

How each donation moves swiftly from surplus to dinner table.

Step 1

Donate

Donors list freshly prepared surplus food with quantity, safe packaging details, and exact pickup address.

Step 2

Match

Local volunteers, charity workers, or families claim available listings directly on the marketplace.

Step 3

Pickup

The receiver visits the location within the allocated safe window, verifies freshness, and collects the meal.

Step 4

Help

Meals are served immediately, bringing relief and dignity while preserving valuable resources and food.

Safety Is Our Top Priority

Food Safety & Handling Standards

Strict hygiene protocols ensure that all shared cooked food is wholesome, clean, and nutritious.

The 2-Hour Window

Cooked food held at room temperature must be consumed or refrigerated within 2 hours of cooking. Never leave hot food in unventilated bags.

Temperature Control

Keep hot dishes above 60°C (140°F) or chill immediately below 5°C (41°F). The danger zone (5°C - 60°C) allows bacterial growth if left unmonitored.

Food-Grade Packaging

Always pack donations in clean, food-grade containers, aluminum foils, or BPA-free boxes. Seal containers tightly to prevent contamination.

Allergen Transparency

Clearly note ingredients in the description field (e.g., peanuts, dairy, gluten, shellfish) to protect receivers with sensitivities.

Personal Hygiene

Wash hands thoroughly with soap for 20 seconds before portioning food. Use clean serving ladles and sanitized surfaces.

What NOT to Donate

Do not donate partly eaten plate scraps, unpasteurized dairy, swollen canned foods, or items that smell sour or rancid.

Engineering & APIs

How 5 Real-World APIs Power Our Platform

We utilize standard JavaScript Fetch API calls to integrate live real-time datasets without backend bloat.

API 1 Live Fetch

Food Details API

Fetches global food categorization, images, and culinary details from TheMealDB and Open Food Facts to automatically standardize food categories.

GET /api/json/v1/1/categories.php
API 2 Live Fetch

Nutrition API

Analyzes macronutrient breakdowns (calories, proteins, carbohydrates, fats) for donated ingredients to help shelters track nutritional balance.

GET /api/v2/product/737628064500.json
API 3 Live Fetch

Recipe API

Suggests inventive leftover transformation recipes (e.g. fried rice, veggie frittatas, bread puddings) to inspire zero-waste home cooking.

GET /recipes?limit=8
API 4 Live Fetch

Location API

Leverages OpenStreetMap Nominatim geo-coordinates to plot community pickup centers, shelters, and food bank dropoff nodes.

GET /nominatim.openstreetmap.org/search
API 5 Live Fetch

Weather API

Queries Open-Meteo real-time temperature and weather conditions to issue smart temperature warnings for outdoor food handovers.

GET /v1/forecast?current_weather=true
Local Engine Client Persistence

LocalStorage Persistence

Synchronizes real-time client donations, pickup requests, status lifecycles, and dashboard KPIs across all 6 pages without requiring server databases.

window.localStorage (CRUD Engine)
